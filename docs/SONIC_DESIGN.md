# Authentic tactile sound direction

Drift uses sound as a material response to motion. It does not add music,
ambient beds, synthetic notification tones, or decorative hover noise.

The intended impression is a film editor handling physical media: cards
sliding, a page turning, cloth catching, leather lifting, a book landing, a
small latch engaging. The sounds are brief enough to preserve narration and
restrained enough that silence remains the dominant state.

## Product rules

1. **Picture remains primary.** Sound follows semantic carousel events; it does
   not drive layout, timing, or export duration.
2. **No surprise audio in the master.** New projects expose tactile preview but
   exported effects are off by default. Projects migrated from schema v1 begin
   with preview and export sound disabled.
3. **No autoplay workaround.** Preview creates and resumes its `AudioContext`
   only inside a trusted pointer or keyboard gesture. Enabling preview and the
   explicit audition button are valid unlock gestures. A muted audition is one
   temporary gesture; it does not silently arm ongoing preview.
4. **No sound-only meaning.** Every cue accompanies a visible state change or
   an existing status message.
5. **No runtime third-party request.** Recordings are committed locally and
   emitted as hashed same-origin WAV assets. They load only after sound is
   intentionally armed, auditioned, or required for export.
6. **One audio master.** Presenter speech and effects become one exact-duration
   stereo master before AAC encoding. Drift never emits competing audio tracks
   or silently drops one.
7. **Deterministic authorship.** Passage inclusion, cue time, sample choice,
   gain, playback rate, and pan derive from saved project state and the pure
   carousel evaluator.
8. **Preview and export speak one language.** Given the same passage sequence,
   seed, density, palette, and variation, both paths choose the same inclusion,
   recording, and playback-rate decision.
9. **Restraint beats coverage.** There are no hover sounds, continuous scrub
   loops, background ambience, or sounds on every slider tick.

## Semantic vocabulary

| Cue | Meaning | Preview | MP4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| passage | A logical slide crosses the viewing threshold | yes | yes |
| grab | The user takes hold of the carousel | yes | no |
| release | The user lets the carousel go | yes | no |
| settle | Motion resolves into a resting state | yes | non-seamless endings |
| control | A discrete editor command changes state | yes | no |
| success | An existing operation completes successfully | yes | no |
| failure | An existing operation fails visibly | yes | no |

Preview passage cues are emitted by `CinematicCarousel` when its logical slot
changes. Export passage cues are generated by `buildSonicTimeline()` from the
same slide geometry, velocity, direction, asset count, seed, and duration used
for picture. Neither path scrapes rendered frames.

A low-discrepancy seeded gate makes Density continuous and monotonic. Increasing
Density only adds passages; it does not replace already accepted ones. Changing
a material palette changes the sound of a passage without changing the authored
rhythm of where passages occur.

## Material palettes

### Studio

Playing-card slides, leather handling, paper, a small metal click, and soft
surface impacts. This is the restrained default: dry, close, and editorial.

### Cinema

Card shoves, wood, metal latches, and slightly firmer impacts. It has more
physical weight without becoming a trailer-effects pack.

### Paper

Page turns, cloth, book movement, and soft landings. It is the least mechanical
palette and suits quiet, tender, archival, or literary decks.

Each semantic cue can draw from multiple authentic recordings. Variation is
therefore not just pitch modulation: deterministic sample selection prevents
the same page turn or card slide from repeating mechanically. Setting Variation
to zero pins every cue to its first recording and neutral playback rate.

## Non-destructive acoustic treatment

The committed CC0 WAV files are source evidence and are never rewritten. Their
upstream Git-blob hashes, SHA-256 values, byte lengths, and licence provenance
remain authoritative.

A separate machine-readable ledger, `src/sonic/assets/treatments.json`, defines
only playback treatment:

- trim start and trim end;
- gain correction in decibels;
- measured active-event RMS;
- measured onset, peak, and treated duration.

The same treatment metadata is used by live preview and offline export. This
removes dead air and normalizes materially different recordings without hiding
or modifying the licensed originals.

`scripts/check-sonic-treatments.mjs` reparses every PCM16 WAV and independently
recomputes the receipts during production build. The gate currently requires:

- all 23 recordings to remain present and uniquely addressed;
- meaningful sound to begin within 50 ms;
- treated active-event energy to remain within a 3.5 dB corpus spread;
- pre-mix peaks to remain below +6 dBFS;
- every declared receipt to match the actual committed source and treatment.

The accepted corpus currently measures a 3.1 dB active-event spread and 44 ms
maximum onset. Those numbers describe the treatment input, not the final master;
semantic family gains, user controls, panning, and the master limiter still
shape the authored result.

## Preview engine

`SonicEngine` owns a lazy Web Audio graph:

- one master gain and one restrained compressor;
- a maximum of eight simultaneous voices;
- per-cue cooldowns;
- continuous seeded density gating for passages;
- separate motion and interface gain families;
- stereo pan derived from horizontal direction;
- short attack and release ramps to prevent clicks;
- visibility suspension and complete teardown on unmount;
- abort-aware asset waits so cancelled work stops promptly while the shared
  local cache remains reusable;
- core passage and settle assets loaded first, with interaction and status cues
  loaded only when needed;
- no decoding of a disabled migrated project's palette until the user opts in.

The engine is suppressed during export so preview feedback cannot leak into
monitoring or the exported master.

## Export engine

`buildSonicTimeline()` produces a deterministic list of passage and optional
settle events. `renderSonicSoundtrack()` renders that list through an exact
48,000 Hz, two-channel `OfflineAudioContext` using the same recording treatments
and passage decisions as preview.

At export:

- sound-only output becomes one AAC track;
- presenter-only output remains one AAC track;
- presenter speech and foley are rendered into one exact-duration continuous
  stereo PCM master before AAC encoding;
- foley therefore survives presenter packet gaps, and mono narration cannot
  collapse lateral motion;
- the under-voice control scales effects only when speech coexists;
- 50/60 fps audio-bearing output fails visibly under the existing codec gate;
- cancellation is checked before decode, during offline work, and before sample
  submission and finalization;
- final output passes container, codec, duration, channel, sample-rate, sync,
  and decoded-audio readback gates.

AAC uses 1,024-sample access units, so decoded container duration may expose no
more than one padded access unit beyond the exact PCM timeline. The PCM master
itself remains exact-length.

## Sources and provenance

All recordings come from Kenney's **Casino Audio**, **RPG Audio**, and
**Impact Sounds** packs, released under CC0 1.0. For reproducibility, Drift
vendors exact files from a pinned revision of the public
`Daarko/sparkstream-sounds` mirror.

`scripts/vendor-sonic-assets.py` verifies each downloaded file against its
expected Git blob SHA-1, verifies RIFF/WAVE headers, computes SHA-256, preserves
the pack licence texts, and writes `src/sonic/assets/manifest.json`.

The manifest records the local path, exact upstream revision and path, Git blob
hash, SHA-256, byte length, source pack, physical material, intended use,
canonical Kenney page, and licence identifier. Production build separately
fails if the WAV corpus disappears, becomes inlined into JavaScript, or stops
being emitted as hashed same-origin assets.

## Tuning boundaries

- Master, passages, controls, density, variation, and under-voice gain are
  normalized from 0 to 1.
- Passage density is deterministic, continuous, monotonic, and independent of
  preview frame rate.
- Cues within 45 ms of a seamless loop boundary are omitted to prevent a
  doubled seam.
- Reduced-motion output is silent.
- Vertical motion remains centred; horizontal motion uses restrained pan.
- No cue is scheduled by `setTimeout()` in exported audio.
- Short-lived preview nodes use bounded gain, pan, rate, cooldown, and polyphony.
- The committed WAV files are never peak-normalized or destructively trimmed.

## Non-goals

- music selection or playback;
- long ambience, room tone, projectors, or film-reel loops;
- generated or synthesized sound effects;
- user-uploaded sample libraries;
- a DAW-style timeline;
- automatic broadcast-loudness mastering;
- sound on hover, every drag pixel, or every range-input event.
